# Interrogate

Help the learner examine their own idea by asking the right questions at the right layer: its framing, its terms, its assumptions, its evidence, its reasoning, its values, or its standpoint. The learner does the answering, and the thinking stays theirs.

**Boundary:** Diagnose what needs examination rather than defaulting to a counterargument, which is what `challenge` does. Ask in manageable stages.

## Start

Follow [Start every task](../explore-mode/SKILL.md#start-every-task). Get the idea in the learner's words and what they want from the conversation: sharper framing, confidence in an argument, or finding the weak spot before someone else does.

## Steps

1. **Diagnose the layer.** Read the idea and decide where examination would help most:
   - **framing:** is this the right question?
   - **definitions:** what do the key terms mean here?
   - **assumptions:** what must be true for this to work?
   - **evidence:** how do you know?
   - **reasoning:** does this follow?
   - **values:** what matters here, and to whom?
   - **standpoint:** from where is this seen?

   Say which layer you're starting with, and why.
2. **Ask one to three questions at a time.** Make them specific, answerable, and open, never leading. Draw on [critical-thinking](../../playbooks/critical-thinking.md), [question-framing](../../playbooks/question-framing.md), and [reflexive-thinking](../../playbooks/reflexive-thinking.md).
3. **Follow the answers.** Go deeper where an answer is vague, contradicts an earlier one, or reveals a new assumption. Move to another layer when this one is resolved or stuck.
4. **Reflect back.** Periodically summarise what the learner has clarified, in their words, and what remains open ([Positionality](../principle-positionality/SKILL.md)).
5. **Optional: several lenses.** For a consequential idea, and where the environment allows, give the idea to separate agents, each with a different lens:
   - evidence and method;
   - consequences;
   - ethics and affected people;
   - cultural context;
   - the definitions and framing themselves.

   Each returns questions, not answers or verdicts. Use different models where available. The lead merges the question sets, removes duplicates, and prioritises them, then continues asking in small stages.
6. **Close the round.** **Checkpoint:** summarise what's been clarified, the assumptions the learner now holds knowingly, and the open questions they may want to research. The learner decides what to change.

## Output

- A running dialogue.
- A closing summary: clarified points, assumptions now explicit, open questions, and suggested next steps (such as `research` or `challenge`).
- Where lenses were used, which lenses and models generated the questions.

## Done when

- The learner has examined at least the most important layer.
- Their answers, not the assistant's, shaped the summary.
- The open questions are explicit.
