# Challenge

Stress-test a position constructively. Find the strongest version of it, then the strongest reasons it might be wrong, and report honestly which of those reasons hold up.

**Boundary:** This is constructive opposition, not disagreement for its own sake. Don't manufacture an opposing case or create false balance ([Preserve Disagreement](../principle-preserve-disagreement/SKILL.md)).

## Start

Follow [Start every task](../explore-mode/SKILL.md#start-every-task). Identify the position to challenge and what it's for: an argument in the learner's work, a decision, a proposal, or a finding from research.

## Steps

1. **State the position at its strongest.** Restate it precisely, with its best supporting reasons. **Checkpoint:** confirm the restatement with the learner. Challenging a misreading wastes everyone's time.
2. **Map what it rests on:** premises, hidden assumptions, and cruxes ([critical-thinking](../../playbooks/critical-thinking.md)).
3. **Generate challenges:**
   - counterarguments;
   - alternative explanations for the same evidence;
   - counter-evidence;
   - contexts where the position fails, including for whom it doesn't hold ([Cultural Awareness](../principle-cultural-awareness/SKILL.md));
   - consequences its advocates overlook ([second-order-thinking](../../playbooks/second-order-thinking.md)).
4. **Delegate for independence.** Where the environment allows:
   - Give the position and its evidence to a separate agent, preferably on a different model, and ask for its strongest challenges. Don't share the learner's preferred answer or your own challenges.
   - Search for disconfirming evidence in parallel ([search](../search/SKILL.md)).

   Where independence isn't available, say the challenge came from the same assistant.
5. **Test the challenges themselves.** Check each for evidence and status ([verify-sources](../../playbooks/verify-sources.md)). Drop or downgrade challenges that are merely rhetorical. Say which challenges fail, as well as which succeed.
6. **Rank by seriousness.** Classify each challenge:
   - **fatal if true;**
   - **weakens the position;**
   - **narrows its scope;**
   - **worth noting.**

   For each, say what would settle it.
7. **Return the challenges.** **Checkpoint:** the learner decides whether to revise, qualify, defend, or abandon the position. Don't do it for them ([Human Judgement](../principle-human-judgement/SKILL.md)).

## Output

- The position as restated and confirmed.
- Challenges ranked by seriousness, each with its evidence status and what would settle it.
- Challenges that failed, and why.
- The source of the challenges: the same assistant, another agent, or another model.
- Questions for the learner.

## Done when

- The position was challenged at its strongest.
- Every challenge carries an evidence status.
- Failed challenges are reported.
- The response to the challenges is left to the learner.
