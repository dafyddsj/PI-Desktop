# Explain

Help a specific audience understand a concept, mechanism, or relationship: clearly, accurately, and at the right level. Check that understanding happened, not just that words were delivered.

**Boundary:** Adapt to the audience. Distinguish useful simplification from misleading certainty.

## Start

Follow [Start every task](../explore-mode/SKILL.md#start-every-task). Establish who the explanation is for (the learner, or an audience the learner is preparing for), why they need it, and what they already know. Infer this from context where possible; ask one question if it matters ([Contextual Adaptation](../principle-contextual-adaptation/SKILL.md)).

## Steps

1. **State the core idea** in one or two plain sentences.
2. **Build it up.** Cover the mechanism (how it works), the key terms (defined once and used consistently), and the boundaries (what it isn't, and where it stops applying).
3. **Use examples well.** Draw them from the learner's field and context where possible, and choose examples that don't default to one culture ([Cultural Awareness](../principle-cultural-awareness/SKILL.md)). Say where each analogy breaks down.
4. **Address misconceptions.** Name the common misunderstandings and why they're tempting.
5. **Mark uncertainty and contest.** Say where experts disagree, where the evidence is thin, and where a simplification leaves out something that matters ([Preserve Uncertainty](../principle-preserve-uncertainty/SKILL.md)).
6. **Verify the facts that matter.** Explanations of emerging technology date quickly. Check consequential facts against sources rather than recall ([verify-sources](../../playbooks/verify-sources.md)).
   - **Delegate:** where the environment allows, a separate agent can fact-check the key claims while you draft.
7. **Check understanding.** Invite the learner to explain it back, apply it to a case, or answer a quick question. Fill the gaps that shows ([Human Judgement](../principle-human-judgement/SKILL.md)).
8. **Point onward.** Offer a few well-chosen sources for going deeper ([suggest](../suggest/SKILL.md)).

## Output

An explanation pitched to the audience: core idea, mechanism, examples, misconceptions, and uncertainty. Include a check-for-understanding prompt and sources. When the audience is the learner's own readers, the explanation is for the learner's understanding. They write their own version for submission ([Learner Authorship](../principle-learner-authorship/SKILL.md)).

## Done when

- The explanation fits the audience.
- Simplifications that drop something important are flagged.
- Key facts are verified.
- Understanding has been checked.
