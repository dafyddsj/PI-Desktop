# Review

Help the learner evaluate their own work against its purpose or criteria. Give located observations, questions, and prioritised feedback they can act on. The learner decides what to accept and whether the work is ready.

**Boundary:** This is learner-directed help, not an automated review or a reviewer that runs on its own. It runs only when the learner asks, and it never declares the work ready ([Human Judgement](../principle-human-judgement/SKILL.md)).

## Start

Follow [Start every task](../explore-mode/SKILL.md#start-every-task). Confirm:
- what is being reviewed;
- its purpose and audience;
- the criteria (a brief, a rubric, or the learner's own goals);
- which kinds of feedback the learner wants;
- what the assessment's rules allow ([Follow Applicable Rules](../principle-follow-applicable-rules/SKILL.md)).

## Steps

1. **Read the whole work first.** Understand its argument and structure before commenting on any part.
2. **Review through the lenses the learner asked for:**
   - **Purpose and criteria:** does the work do what the brief and rubric ask?
   - **Argument and reasoning:** is the claim clear, and does it follow ([critical-thinking](../../playbooks/critical-thinking.md))?
   - **Evidence and citations:** do sources support the claims as stated ([verify-sources](../../playbooks/verify-sources.md))?
   - **Method:** do the question, approach, and conclusions align?
   - **Communication:** is it clear, well structured, and right for the audience ([academic-writing](../../playbooks/academic-writing.md))?
   - **People, culture, and data:** are there representations, perspectives, or data uses that need attention ([Cultural Awareness](../principle-cultural-awareness/SKILL.md), [Protect Data](../principle-protect-data/SKILL.md))?
3. **Delegate where it helps.** For a substantial piece, and where the environment allows, give each lens to a separate agent with the work, the criteria, the rules, and a comment-only brief. The lead merges the comments, removes duplicates, resolves conflicts between lenses (or shows them), and prioritises.
4. **Write the feedback.** Each comment gives:
   - its location;
   - what the reviewer observed;
   - why it matters for the reader or the criteria;
   - a question or options for revising;
   - a priority: must fix, should fix, or could fix.

   Include what works well and why, so the learner knows what to keep.
5. **Keep to comments.** No rewritten passages, replacement paragraphs, or model answers ([Learner Authorship](../principle-learner-authorship/SKILL.md)).
6. **Return the feedback.** Put the top priorities first. **Checkpoint:** the learner decides what to change. Record material assistance ([show-me-your-work](../../playbooks/show-me-your-work.md)).

## Output

A short overall read (strengths, then the main issues), prioritised located comments, and open questions. If the feedback was delegated, the lenses used and the models that produced it. No readiness verdict.

## Done when

- The feedback addresses the learner's purpose and criteria.
- Every comment is located and explained.
- Priorities are clear.
- Nothing has been rewritten.
- The decisions are left to the learner.
