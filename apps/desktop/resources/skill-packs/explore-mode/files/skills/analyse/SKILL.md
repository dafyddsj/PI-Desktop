# Analyse

Examine material, data, or an issue closely. What is actually there, what patterns and relationships appear, what assumptions sit underneath, and what follows? Keep what you observed apart from what you interpret.

**Boundary:** Distinguish observations from interpretations. Identify evidence gaps before proposing more research.

## Start

Follow [Start every task](../explore-mode/SKILL.md#start-every-task). Establish what is being analysed and for what purpose. Check where the material came from and whether it may be used this way, especially personal, workplace, or community data ([Protect Data](../principle-protect-data/SKILL.md)).

## Steps

1. **Inventory the material:** what it is, where it came from, when, who produced it, and what it covers and leaves out. For data, record its structure, size, missing values, and how it was collected.
2. **Observe.** Describe what is present without explaining it: recurring themes, frequencies, contrasts, and outliers, each with a locator (page, row, timestamp, quote).
3. **Interpret.** Offer readings of the patterns, clearly labelled as interpretations. Give alternatives where the evidence allows more than one reading ([critical-thinking](../../playbooks/critical-thinking.md)).
4. **Surface assumptions.** Identify the assumptions in the material itself, in how it was produced, and in the analysis so far.
5. **Apply methods only where they fit.** Use [systems-thinking](../../playbooks/systems-thinking.md) for interacting causes, [second-order-thinking](../../playbooks/second-order-thinking.md) for consequences, [evidence-synthesis](../../playbooks/evidence-synthesis.md) for several sources, and [five-whys](../../playbooks/five-whys.md) for a local cause. For data, keep descriptive findings apart from inferential claims, and don't claim more than the data supports.
6. **Delegate where it helps:**
   - **Large material:** split it into parts. Each agent applies the same analytic frame and writes its own observations. The lead integrates the parts and checks consistency across them.
   - **Interpretive work:** have a second agent, ideally on another model, analyse the same material independently. Where the two analyses differ, that shows where the interpretation is fragile.
7. **Name the implications and the gaps.** What follows if the interpretations hold? What evidence is missing that would change them? Identify these gaps before proposing further research.
8. **Return the analysis.** **Checkpoint:** the learner judges which interpretations they find convincing.

## Output

- Observations with locators.
- Interpretations, labelled, with alternatives.
- Assumptions.
- Implications.
- Evidence gaps.
- How the work was executed, if delegated.

Material contributions go in the records ([show-me-your-work](../../playbooks/show-me-your-work.md)).

## Done when

- Observations and interpretations are visibly separate.
- Every pattern points back to the material.
- Alternatives and gaps are named.
- Any independent analyses are compared, not merged silently.
